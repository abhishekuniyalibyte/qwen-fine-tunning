I am building a closed-book exam about one chapter of a public-domain
book, for a fine-tuning experiment. The exam will be given to a language
model WITHOUT the book, and its answers will be graded against your
reference answers and key points.

Book: The Handbook of Soap Manufacture, by W. H. Simmons and H. A. Appleton (1908)
Chapter: X — Analysis of Raw Materials, Soap, and Glycerine
Attached: the full text of this chapter only.

Use only the attached chapter. Do not use outside knowledge, and do not
use anything from earlier conversations.

Task: write 22 FINAL questions and 8 DEV questions (30 in total) that
test knowledge stated in this chapter.

Rules for every question:
1. Start the question with exactly:
   "In The Handbook of Soap Manufacture by W. H. Simmons and H. A. Appleton, "
   and then continue the same sentence in lower case (for example
   "..., what temperature ..." or "..., how did ..."), not with a
   capital letter.
2. Book-specific: ask for something only a reader of this chapter would
   know: an exact quantity, temperature, strength or proportion; a step
   or the order of steps in a described process; a reason or condition
   the authors give; a distinction or comparison they draw; a named
   method, substance or person; the authors' own recommendation or
   opinion. Avoid anything a general chemistry or soap-making textbook
   would answer the same way, and avoid well-known facts (famous
   chemists, discoveries, dates, standard chemistry) that a well-read
   model could answer without this book. Examples of questions NOT to
   write: how Berthelot synthesised fats; what beeswax or spermaceti
   chiefly consist of; which acids are in a common oil; what
   saponification produces; what caustic soda is made from; which
   carbonate to add when a potash lye lacks carbonate. Also avoid any
   question whose answer can be worked out from the question itself by
   basic chemistry or common sense.
   Test every question: if a well-read chemist could answer it fully
   without this book, drop it and write another. Prefer the authors'
   own figures, conditions, comparisons, recommendations and the
   lesser-known people, patents and experiments they cite.
   This chapter describes laboratory analysis, and many of its methods
   are standard analytical chemistry. Do not ask about standard
   procedures or textbook facts, for example: weighing sodium as
   sodium sulphate; the crystal forms that distinguish cholesterol from
   phytosterol; linalol or terpineol decomposing during acetylation;
   what a saponification, iodine or acid value measures; the colour of
   the stain in the Gutzeit arsenic test; which indicator suits which
   titration; lead acetate paper as the test for sulphides; carbon
   tetrachloride as the solvent in Wijs's method. In general, do not
   ask for the classic reagent, solvent or indicator of a named or
   standard method, or for a test whose answer follows from basic
   chemistry once the substance is named. Ask instead about the
   authors' own sample weights, volumes, times, temperatures, factors
   and limits, the particular modifications and preferences they
   state, and the results of their own examinations.
3. Answerable from one short passage: the full answer must be supported
   by 1-3 consecutive sentences of the chapter.
4. Paraphrase: do not copy the book's wording into the question. Ask in
   your own words, so the question tests knowledge, not memorised text.
5. One clear answer: if the book's answer depends on a condition, put
   the condition in the question. No yes/no questions, no multiple
   choice, no questions with unrelated parts. Do not hint at the
   answer: the question must not state or imply any key point, and
   must not spell out the parts the answer should contain (not
   "including the solution, its strength and its performance"). The
   answer must not be contained in the question's own wording (not
   "how does the mould allow for different tablet thicknesses?" when
   the answer is "it is adjustable").
6. Self-contained: never say "this chapter", "the above", "the
   following", and do not refer to figures or tables by number.
7. Prose only: base questions on the running text, not on large tables,
   figure captions or the list of section titles at the top.
8. Spread: cover the chapter from beginning to end and mix the kinds of
   question in rule 2. No two questions (final or dev) may ask about the
   same fact or use the same or overlapping supporting_passage, and no
   question may give away the answer to another.
9. Final and dev questions are equally hard and spread over the same
   chapter; do not put the easy ones in one group.
10. Already used: these facts were asked in an earlier test and must
    NOT be asked again, from any angle, and their sentences must not be
    used as a supporting_passage:
    - In the separate soap determinations, which indicator is used to titrate free caustic alkali in the alcoholic filtrate, and which is used for free carbonated alkali in the aqueous extract of the filter residue?
      (passage starts: "_Free caustic alkali_ is estimated by dissolving 2 grammes of the soap, ...")
    - In Halphen's test for cotton-seed oil fatty acids, what are the two successive heating stages, and what colour denotes the presence of cotton-seed oil?
      (passage starts: "_Halphen's Reaction._--This is a special test to determine the presence or absence ...")

For each question give:
- required_key_points: 1-3 short facts that a fully correct answer MUST
  contain (numbers and units exactly as in the book). Only a fully
  correct answer counts, so:
  - each key point must be something the question explicitly asks for;
  - never repeat information that is already in the question;
  - never add a conclusion, reason or explanation the question does not
    ask for;
  - never add a key point that only follows from another key point by
    chemistry or logic (not "ammonia is liberated" after "an ammoniacal
    salt is used");
  - leave out minor or obvious items a correct answer could reasonably
    omit (for example, "water" in a list of ingredients); if a detail
    matters, ask for it explicitly in the question;
  - when the book gives items only as examples ("such as", "names such
    as ...", "etc."), do not require all of them: write the key point
    as "any of X, Y or Z".
- reference_answer: one or two sentences containing all the key points.
- supporting_passage: the 1-3 consecutive sentences from the chapter
  that support the answer, copied verbatim, character for character.
  No "..." and no paraphrase.
- section: the nearest section heading, as written in the chapter.
- kind: one of quantity, procedure, reason, distinction, definition, other.
