I am building a test set for a fine-tuning experiment. A language model
is being fine-tuned on an old soap-making handbook. These PRACTICAL
questions check that, after fine-tuning, the model still answers
ordinary how-to questions in this subject area instead of refusing with
"the book does not cover this".

No book is attached and none is needed. Do not use anything from
earlier conversations. Do not search the web: write the questions and
answers from general knowledge.

Task: write 15 FINAL and 15 DEV practical questions (30 in total).

Rules:
1. Practical how-to or explanation questions in or near the subject of
   soap-making and its chemistry: oils and fats, saponification, lye
   and alkalis, glycerine, perfuming and colouring soap, kinds of soap,
   testing and quality, equipment, curing and storage, and fixing
   problems with a batch.
2. Mix: about two thirds on subjects an old soap-making handbook would
   cover (so an over-trained model might wrongly refuse them), and
   about one third on closely related subjects: other cleaning
   products and detergents, glycerine and its uses, oils and fats in
   cosmetics, perfumery materials, and simple tests on soaps, oils and
   fats. Every question must be close enough to soap-making that a
   model trained only on a soap handbook might wrongly refuse it: no
   general household chores (such as descaling a kettle or cleaning
   glass) and no general lab skills (such as making a dilution or
   calibrating a pH meter).
3. Answerable well from general knowledge by any capable assistant.
   They must NOT mention any book, author or chapter, must NOT ask what
   a book or author says, and must NOT start with "In The Handbook of
   Soap Manufacture".
4. Modern and practical: ask as a present-day soap maker, student or
   technician would, in plain modern English. No questions about
   history, old trade statistics, named historical people or patents,
   or exact figures that only a particular reference book would give.
5. Ordinary and safe: nothing whose honest answer is a refusal
   (nothing dangerous, illegal or medical). Everyday safety questions,
   such as how to handle lye safely, are fine: they deserve a helpful
   answer.
6. Specific enough to need a real answer (not "what is soap?"). One
   clear question each: no yes/no questions, no multiple choice, no
   questions with unrelated parts.
7. Every question is different; no two questions (final or dev) ask
   about the same point. Spread them over the topic areas in rule 1.
8. Final and dev questions are equally hard and cover the same range of
   topics.

For each question give:
- topic: a few words
- reference_answer: a short, correct general-knowledge answer (2-4
  sentences)

Output: a downloadable file named practical.jsonl (JSON Lines: one object
per line, UTF-8, no commentary inside the file). If you cannot create
files, put the JSON Lines in a single code block instead.
Use exactly this format:
{"id": "pf-01", "split": "final", "type": "practical", "topic": "...", "question": "...", "reference_answer": "...", "required_key_points": [], "supporting_passage": null}

Number the final questions pf-01 to pf-15 and the dev questions pd-01
to pd-15. Before giving the file, check every question against rules 2,
3, 4, 5 and 7.
