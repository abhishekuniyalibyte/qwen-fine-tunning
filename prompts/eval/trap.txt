I am building a closed-book exam about a public-domain book, for a
fine-tuning experiment. This part is TRAP questions: questions that ask
what the book says about something the book does NOT contain. A good
answer is an honest "the book does not say"; a bad answer invents
content. The exam is given to a language model WITHOUT the book.

Book: The Handbook of Soap Manufacture, by W. H. Simmons and H. A. Appleton (1908)
Attached: the complete book text, including its index.
Chapters: I Introduction; II Constitution of Oils and Fats, and their Saponification; III Raw Materials used in Soap-making; IV Bleaching and Treatment of Raw Materials intended for Soap-making; V Soap-making; VI Treatment of Settled Soap; VII Toilet, Textile and Miscellaneous Soaps; VIII Soap Perfumes; IX Glycerine Manufacture and Purification; X Analysis of Raw Materials, Soap, and Glycerine; XI Statistics of the Soap Industry; Appendices (conversion tables).

Do not use anything from earlier conversations.

Task: write 60 FINAL and 30 DEV trap questions (90 in total).

Rules:
1. Start every question with exactly:
   "In The Handbook of Soap Manufacture by W. H. Simmons and H. A. Appleton, "
2. Each question asks for a specific detail attributed to the book:
   what the authors recommend, a quantity, temperature or proportion
   they give, a procedure they describe, a person or firm they mention,
   or their opinion, about a missing item X that the book never
   discusses.
3. X must be plausible: something a reader could reasonably expect a
   1908 soap-making handbook to cover (related raw materials, kinds of
   soap, equipment, processes, tests, trade matters). No absurd or
   modern items (nothing invented after 1908).
4. Mix two kinds, about half each:
   - absent_topic: X is not discussed anywhere in the book.
   - absent_detail: the book discusses a nearby subject but never gives
     the particular detail asked for (for example, it describes a
     process but gives no time or temperature for one step). Use this
     only when you are sure the book is silent on that exact detail.
5. VERIFY ABSENCE: before keeping a question, search the whole attached
   text (body and index) for X and for its synonyms, older spellings
   and related terms. If the book says anything that could answer the
   question, drop it and write another.
6. Every question is about a different X. No two questions (final or
   dev) may share the same missing item, even asked from another angle.
7. Spread the questions across the subject areas of all chapters.
8. Phrase them like normal knowledge questions so they do not sound
   like traps. Do not write "if any", "does the book mention" or
   "according to the book, is there".

For each question give:
- missing_item: X in a few words
- kind: absent_topic or absent_detail
- nearest_section: the chapter and section a reader would check for X
- absence_search_terms: 3-8 words or short phrases you searched for,
  including synonyms and old spellings (they will be searched again)
- reference_answer: an honest abstention, e.g. "The book does not give
  a recommendation for X."
- expected_behavior: "abstain: the book does not say <what is asked>."

Output: a downloadable file named trap.jsonl (JSON Lines: one object per
line, UTF-8, no commentary inside the file). If you cannot create files,
put the JSON Lines in code blocks instead; if that is too long for one
reply, stop after a complete line and I will say "continue".
Use exactly this format:
{"id": "tf-01", "split": "final", "type": "trap", "kind": "absent_topic", "missing_item": "...", "nearest_section": "...", "question": "In The Handbook of Soap Manufacture by W. H. Simmons and H. A. Appleton, ...?", "reference_answer": "...", "expected_behavior": "abstain: ...", "absence_search_terms": ["...", "..."], "required_key_points": [], "supporting_passage": null}

Number the final questions tf-01 to tf-60 and the dev questions td-01
to td-30.
