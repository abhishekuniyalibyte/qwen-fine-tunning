I am building a closed-book exam about a public-domain book, for a
fine-tuning experiment. This part is TRAP questions: questions that ask
what the book says about something the book does NOT contain. A good
answer is an honest "the book does not say"; a bad answer invents
content. The exam is given to a language model WITHOUT the book.

Book: The Handbook of Soap Manufacture, by W. H. Simmons and H. A. Appleton (1908)
Attached: the complete book text, including its index. The Project
Gutenberg notices at the start and end, and the publisher's
advertisements after "THE END.", are not part of the book: ignore them.
Chapters: I Introduction; II Constitution of Oils and Fats, and their Saponification; III Raw Materials used in Soap-making; IV Bleaching and Treatment of Raw Materials intended for Soap-making; V Soap-making; VI Treatment of Settled Soap; VII Toilet, Textile and Miscellaneous Soaps; VIII Soap Perfumes; IX Glycerine Manufacture and Purification; X Analysis of Raw Materials, Soap, and Glycerine; XI Statistics of the Soap Industry; Appendices (conversion tables).

Do not use anything from earlier conversations. Do not search the web
or download anything: use only the attached file.

Task: write 60 FINAL and 30 DEV trap questions (90 in total).

Rules:
1. Start every question with exactly:
   "In The Handbook of Soap Manufacture by W. H. Simmons and H. A. Appleton, "
   and then continue the same sentence in lower case (for example
   "..., what temperature ..." or "..., how do the authors ..."), not
   with a capital letter.
2. Each question asks for a specific detail attributed to the book:
   what the authors recommend, a quantity, temperature or proportion
   they give, a procedure they describe, a person, firm or patent they
   mention, or their opinion, about a missing item X that the book
   never discusses.
3. X must be plausible: something a reader could reasonably expect a
   1908 soap-making handbook to cover (related raw materials, kinds of
   soap, equipment, processes, tests, trade matters). No absurd or
   modern items (nothing invented or named after 1908).
4. Two kinds, about two thirds and one third:
   - absent_topic (about 40 final and 20 dev): X is not discussed
     anywhere in the book. If the book has a passage on the material,
     process, place or person concerned (for example, a country's soap
     industry) and only the asked-for detail is missing, the kind is
     absent_detail, not absent_topic.
   - absent_detail (about 20 final and 10 dev): the book discusses a
     nearby subject but never gives the particular detail asked for
     (for example, it describes a process but gives no time or
     temperature for one step). Use this only when you are sure the
     book is silent on that exact detail, and name the passage where
     the subject is discussed in nearest_section.
5. VERIFY ABSENCE: before keeping a question, search the whole book
   (body and index) for X and for its synonyms, older spellings, plural
   forms and related terms. If the book says anything that could answer
   the question, even in passing, drop it and write another. Also
   drop it if the book gives something a model could quote as a close
   answer: results or figures on the same subject (for example, the
   results of a germicidal test when the question asks for the test
   procedure), or a closely related substance (for example, saponin or
   Quillaia bark when the question asks about soapwort).
6. Every question is about a different X. No two questions (final or
   dev) may share the same missing item, even under another name or
   asked from another angle (not one trap on converting Clark's degrees
   of water hardness and another on the maximum Clark hardness of
   water: both are about Clark water hardness).
7. Spread the questions across the subject areas of all eleven
   chapters, roughly in proportion to their length.
8. Phrase them like normal knowledge questions, so they do not sound
   like traps and do not hint that the book is silent. Do not write
   "if any", "does the book mention", "according to the book, is
   there" or "if the authors discuss it".
9. One clear question: no yes/no questions, no multiple choice, no
   questions with unrelated parts. Self-contained: never say "this
   chapter", "the above" or "the following", and do not refer to
   figures or tables by number.
10. Final and dev questions are equally plausible and spread over the
    same subject areas; do not put the easy ones in one group.

For each question give:
- missing_item: X in a few words
- kind: absent_topic or absent_detail
- nearest_section: the chapter and section a reader would check for X
- absence_search_terms: 3-8 words or short phrases you searched for,
  including synonyms, old spellings and plural forms (they will be
  searched again)
- reference_answer: an honest abstention, e.g. "The book does not give
  a recommendation for X."
- expected_behavior: "abstain: the book does not say <what is asked>."

Output: a downloadable file named trap.jsonl (JSON Lines: one object per
line, UTF-8, no commentary inside the file). If you cannot create files,
put the JSON Lines in code blocks instead; if that is too long for one
reply, stop after a complete line and I will say "continue".
Use exactly this format:
{"id": "tf-01", "split": "final", "type": "trap", "kind": "absent_topic", "missing_item": "...", "nearest_section": "...", "question": "In The Handbook of Soap Manufacture by W. H. Simmons and H. A. Appleton, ...?", "reference_answer": "...", "expected_behavior": "abstain: ...", "absence_search_terms": ["...", "..."], "required_key_points": [], "supporting_passage": null}

Number the final questions tf-01 to tf-60 and the dev questions td-01
to td-30. Before giving the file, search the book once more for every
missing_item and its absence_search_terms, replace any question the book
could answer, and check every question against rules 1, 4, 5, 6, 8
and 9.
